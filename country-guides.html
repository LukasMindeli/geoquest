Country atlas

A practical atlas for remembering countries on the globe.

The WorldOrWrong atlas is built for active recall. A country card is not just a list of trivia: it gives you a compact route for memory - where to look, which capital anchors the country, which region it belongs to, and which visual detail can make it easier to recognize next time.

Start with regions

Regions reduce the size of the problem. If you know that Georgia is in Asia, Portugal is in Europe, and Ghana is in Africa, the globe becomes searchable instead of overwhelming.

Use capitals as anchors

Capitals are strong memory hooks when they are connected to countries. Tbilisi anchors Georgia, Ottawa anchors Canada, Brasilia anchors Brazil, and Astana anchors Kazakhstan.

Separate facts from clues

Languages and currencies are useful reference facts, but they are often too broad for fair guessing. The game tries to use precise clues, while the atlas keeps the wider context.

Example learning paths

GeorgiaLook in the Caucasus region. Tbilisi is the capital, Georgian is the official language, and the Georgian lari is the currency. On the globe, it helps to find the Black Sea and the mountain belt between Europe and Asia.
JapanLook east of the Korean Peninsula. Tokyo is the capital. The island chain shape makes Japan easier to recognize once you remember its north-south curve.
BrazilStart with South America. Brazil is the largest country on the continent, Brasilia is the capital, and the Amazon region is one of the major geographic anchors.
EgyptFind northeast Africa and the Nile. Cairo is the capital, and the country links Africa, the Mediterranean and the Red Sea in a way that is easy to remember visually.
CanadaUse North America as the starting region. Canada stretches across the north of the continent; Ottawa is the capital, and the country is easier to spot by its size and Arctic coastline.
New ZealandLook southeast of Australia. Wellington is the capital. The two main islands make the country a strong visual target once you know where Oceania is.

How the atlas supports the game

The atlas gives players a slower place to check facts before returning to timed play. If a player repeatedly misses a country, the best next step is to open the card, read the region and capital, then view the country on the globe. That loop turns a failed answer into a useful map memory.

WorldOrWrong also keeps educational clarity in mind. It avoids presenting sensitive political claims as game prompts, and it treats country facts as reference material rather than arguments. The goal is to help players learn geography in a neutral, practical way.

Suggested practice routine

  1. Choose one region in the atlas and read five country cards.
  2. Open the globe viewer and rotate to those countries.
  3. Play a short solo round with the same region selected.
  4. Review missed countries again in the atlas.
  5. Repeat the same region the next day before moving to the whole world.
